📌 Legal Liability Notice for Certificate of Translation

The Certificate of Translation is an official legal process certifying translation accuracy. Its legal scope is explicitly defined as follows:

  • • This document certifies that the translation was completed at the applicant's request and accurately corresponds to the original text.
  • • The translator assumes no legal liability for any forgery, alteration, or fundamental authenticity of the original source document.

In summary, Foreign Language Administrative Attorneys do not adjudicate the underlying authenticity of source documents, but rather officially certify that the translated text is complete, true, and accurate to the provided original document.

Comparison: Translation Cert vs Notarization vs Apostille

Each certificate serves distinct legal authentication functions. Always verify submission guidelines with your receiving authority.

Type Authorized Body What It Certifies When Required
Certificate of Translation Foreign Language Admin. Attorney Exact match of translation with source text & translator qualifications When receiving body asks for qualified/certified translation
Translation Notarization Licensed Notary Public / Law Firm Notary's official seal on translator statement When receiving authority explicitly mandates 'Notarization'
Apostille Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) Authenticity of official seals and signatures on public records When submitting docs to Apostille Convention member states
Consular Authentication MOFA & Foreign Embassy Consulates Final verification of validity for overseas document submission When submitting docs to Non-Apostille member states

Process Comparison & Apostille Country Status

Standard Consular Process (Non-Member States) Apostille Convention Process (Streamlined)
Document Issuance → MOFA Confirmation → Foreign Embassy Legalization → Recognition in Target Nation Document Issuance → MOFA Apostille Issuance → Immediate Recognition in Member States
